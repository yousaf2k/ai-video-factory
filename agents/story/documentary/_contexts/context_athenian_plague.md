# Context: The Plague of Athens (430-426 BCE)

## Era Overview
A devastating epidemic that struck Athens during the second year of the Peloponnesian War, directly influencing the city's eventual defeat and the loss of its greatest leader, Pericles.

## Historical Facts & Timeline

### Origins and Spread (430 BCE)
- **Year 2 of the War**: Shortly after the Spartan invasion of Attica.
- **Overcrowding**: Rural populations had fled behind the "Long Walls" into the city, creating catastrophic sanitation conditions.
- **Piraeus Gateway**: The disease first appeared in the harbor, then moved into the city.
- **Thucydides**: The primary historical source, who contracted the disease and survived.

### Medical Symptoms (Thucydides' Analysis)
- **Sudden Onset**: Intense heat in the head, inflamed and bloody throat/tongue.
- **Progression**: Violent coughing, chest pain, and retching.
- **Physical Toll**: Reddish/livid skin with blisters and ulcers; extreme internal heat and insatiable thirst.
- **Fatality**: Most died within 7-9 days; survivors often lost extremities (fingers/toes) or memory.

### The Social Collapse
- **Moral Anomie**: Breakdown of law and religious fear; people indulged in immediate pleasure, believing they would die soon.
- **Religious Despair**: Temples became cluttered with corpses as traditional burial rites (washing, funeral pyres) were impossible to maintain.
- **Caregiver Mortality**: Early physicians and family members died more frequently due to exposure.

### Death of Pericles (429 BCE)
- **The Turning Point**: Loss of Athens' most capable strategist and political leader.
- **Family Loss**: Pericles lost his legitimate sons to the same plague before succumbing himself.

## High-Fidelity Sensory Details
- **Visuals**: Overcrowded shrines filled with sick refugees, mass burial pits (kerameikos), the gaunt face of Pericles on his deathbed, triremes sitting idle in the harbor.
- **Sounds**: The coughing of thousands, the cries of bereaved families, the silence of a city that had lost its confidence.
- **Smells**: The stench of unburied bodies, the smell of medicinal herbs and incense that failed to ward off the disease.
