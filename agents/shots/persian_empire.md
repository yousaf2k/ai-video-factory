## Historical Context & Accuracy

### Time Period
- **Focus**: Achaemenid Empire - c. 550-330 BCE
- **Key Events**:
  - 550 BCE: Cyrus the Great conquers Media
  - 539 BCE: Fall of Babylon to Cyrus
  - 522-486 BCE: Reign of Darius I (building of Persepolis)
  - 480 BCE: Xerxes' invasion of Greece
  - 330 BCE: Fall of Persepolis to Alexander the Great

### Architecture & Urban Development

**Persepolis (Parsa):**
- **Apadana**: Monumental audience hall of Darius I, massive 20m high fluted columns
- **Gate of All Nations**: Monumental gateway built by Xerxes, flanked by colossal Lamassu (bull-men)
- **Tachara**: The private palace of Darius, polished black limestone windows
- **Hundred-Column Hall**: Throne room begun by Xerxes
- **Architectural Details**: Bull-headed capitals, lotus flowers, glazed brick friezes, monumental relief staircases showing tribute bearers.

**Other Centers:**
- **Pasargadae**: Cyrus' capital, modest palace, four-fold gardens (chahar bagh), Tomb of Cyrus (stepped pyramid base)
- **Susa**: Winter capital, famous for polychrome glazed brick friezes of lions and archers
- **Ecbatana**: Summer capital
- **Naqsh-e Rustam**: Rock-cut royal tombs carved high into cliffs in the shape of crosses

### Clothing & Appearance

**Royal Attire:**
- **King of Kings**: Elaborate pleated long robe (candys) dyed rich purple and gold
- **Crown/Tiara**: Kidaris (tall structured royal tiara), gold diadems
- **Jewelry**: Heavy gold torcs (neck rings with animal heads), armbands, earrings
- **Grooming**: Exquisitely curled, long square beards and curled hair perfumed with expensive oils

**Military and Elite (Immortals/Nobles):**
- **Median Dress**: Trousers (anaxyrides), long-sleeved tunics, soft felt cap (basbash) covering mouth
- **Persian Dress**: Flowing pleated robes (often worn over trousers)
- **Colors**: Vibrant yellows, purples, blues, reds, intricate woven patterns (rosettes, lions)
- **Footwear**: Soft leather shoes or boots with slightly upturned toes

**Weapons & Armor:**
- **Immortals (Melophoroi)**: Carrying spears with golden or silver apple-shaped counterweights
- **Sparabara (Shield Bearers)**: Large rectangular wicker shields (spara), short spears
- **Archers**: Mastery of the composite bow (carried in a gorytos quiver)
- **Armor**: Scale armor (often worn invisibly under robes), rarely heavy plate
- **Cavalry**: Fast light cavalry, scythed chariots (blades on wheels)
- **Swords**: Akinakes (short dagger/sword) worn suspended from the right thigh

### Material Culture & Objects

**Treasures:**
- **Rhytons**: Horn-shaped drinking vessels made of solid gold or silver ending in animal protomes (lions, ibexes, griffins)
- **Phialae**: Shallow gold and silver drinking bowls with rosette patterns
- **Cylinder Seals**: Used to mark clay tablets, often showing the King wrestling beasts
- **Daric**: Gold coin introduced by Darius showing the King as an kneeling archer

### Social Structure & Religion

**The Court and Empire:**
- **Shahanshah**: The ultimate autocrat, surrounded by immense ritual protocol (proskynesis)
- **Satraps**: Provincial governors ruling immense territories with great wealth
- **Royal Road**: Excellent highway system with posting stations (angareion) for rapid messengers

**Zoroastrianism (Early):**
- **Ahura Mazda**: The supreme god of light and truth, symbolized by the Faravahar (winged disc with a human figure)
- **Fire Altars**: Open-air platforms where sacred fires burned continuously
- **Magi**: Priestly class, wearing distinct hoods covering the mouth to avoid polluting the sacred fire

## Prompt Guidelines

### 1. First-Person Perspective
Always describe the scene as if you are there photographing it:
- "Standing on the Apadana staircase in Persepolis, I photographed..."
- "I captured the golden Immortals marching from Susa..."
- "Walking through the gardens of Pasargadae, I witnessed..."

### 2. Authentic Details (Critical)
- ❌ **Don't include**: Islamic minarets, Arab clothing, turbans, curved scimitars, Roman legionaries
- ✅ **Do include**: "Bull-headed column capitals," "pleated purple robes," "gold ibex rhytons," "wicker spara shields," "Ahura Mazda winged disc"

### 3. Professional Photography Style
- "Shot on Hasselblad medium format," "85mm portrait lens," "cinematic lighting," "ultra-detailed textures of gold and lapis lazuli."

### 4. Lighting & Color Palette
- **Colors**: Rich imperial purple, brilliant gold, lapis lazuli blue, turquoise glazed brick, desert ochre
- **Lighting**: "Intense Iranian plateau sunlight," "deep shadows of the Apadana columns," "golden hour hitting the Zagros mountains," "flickering light of Zoroastrian fire altars."

## Video & Motion Prompt Guidelines (For Wan 2.2 I2V)

The `motion_prompt` must NOT just be camera directions. It must describe a living, moving scene to guide the video generation model.

When writing the `motion_prompt`, follow these strict rules:

1. **scene_id:** MUST be the 0-based index of the scene this shot belongs to (from the input).
2. **Summarize the Scene:** Briefly state what is in the shot (e.g., "A view of Persepolis...", "The Immortals marching...").
3. **Subject Motion (Verbs):** Describe exactly how the people/animals are moving. Use dynamic verbs (e.g., "Tribute bearers climb the stairs," "The King raises a golden rhyton," "Cavalry charges with bows drawn").
4. **Environmental Motion:** Describe how the elements react (e.g., "Silken canopies flap in the mountain wind," "Incense smoke drifts," "Dust kicks up under chariot wheels").
5. **Camera Movement:** End with the camera instruction (e.g., "The camera slowly pans across the Apadana," "Static camera tracking the King").
6. **NO PHOTOGRAPHY TERMS:** You MUST strip out all static photography terms from the motion prompt. Do NOT include terms like "8K", "50mm", "f/8", "DSLR". Use cinematic pacing terms instead ("cinematic pacing," "fluid motion," "natural movement").

## Camera Types

- **static**: For royal portraits, banquets, static guards
- **slow pan**: For sweeping views of Persepolis or the royal road
- **walk**: For moving through palaces or gardens
- **tracking**: For chariots, cavalry, or marching armies
- **drone**: For wide shots of armies, Pasargadae gardens, or mountain passes
- **orbit**: For showcasing architecture, tombs, or monumental gates
- **zoom**: For dramatic focusing on the King or intricate goldwork
